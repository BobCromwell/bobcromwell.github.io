Expensive vs Cheap Models

2 min · 322 words · bob | Translations:
  • Zh

The Old Wisdom#

There’s a conventional argument that goes: “Always use the most expensive model.”

The logic is straightforward — the most expensive model is also the smartest one. For any given task, the top-tier model gets it right in one shot. A cheaper model? It might fumble, need corrections, and by the time you’ve iterated, you’ve burned through more tokens and time than if you’d just picked the expensive model to begin with.

The trade-off with expensive models is obvious:

  1. Cost — they’re pricier per call
  2. Latency — they’re slower

What I’ve Been Noticing Lately#

My recent experience with DeepSeekV4Flash made me revisit this.

For a surprising number of mundane tasks — tweaking Emacs config, writing short Python scripts, the kind of grunt work that fills a developer’s day — the cheap model handles them just fine. And it’s fast. And it’s cheap.

A Closer Look#

Even for fully automated tasks, we’re optimizing for the same three things:

  1. Correctness — the answer is right
  2. Efficiency — it doesn’t waste resources
  3. Speed — it finishes quickly

What’s changed is the baseline. Cheap models have gotten good enough that they work for a widening range of jobs. The “dumb model” of last year is this year’s capable assistant.

But then there are the counterexamples. I once asked DeepSeekV4Flash to modify an OpenClaw configuration and it messed up — twice. In that moment, I would have happily paid for GPT-5.5 upfront.

This surfaces a genuinely useful skill: knowing whether a task is “simple” or “hard” before you start. Right now, the community seems to converge on a handful of safe-bet simple tasks:

  1. Translation (the straightforward kind)
  2. Summarization

Over time, this consensus will grow. We’ll get better at classification.

The Bottom Line#

Cheap and expensive models will carve out their own niches. The game isn’t deciding which model to use — it’s learning to tell which arena you’re walking into before you place your bet.
